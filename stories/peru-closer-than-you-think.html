Peru is closer than you think

Flight times, prices and the new Lima airport.

Lucas
September 2026 · 2 min read

When people find out I help folks buy property in Peru, the first thing I hear is almost always some version of, "I'd love to, but that's such a huge trip." There's this idea that seeing Peru means blocking off two months, taking the vacation of a lifetime, and building your whole year around it. I get it. South America sounds far. But the truth is, Peru is a lot closer than you think. In many cases, it's no farther than flying across your own country.

It's the same as crossing the U.S.

Let's talk real numbers. A nonstop flight from Miami to Lima is about five hours and fifty-five minutes. From Atlanta, it's roughly six hours and forty-five minutes to seven. Now compare that to a flight within the United States: Seattle to Miami is about six hours. Los Angeles to New York is around five and a half.

In other words, flying from a U.S. hub down to Lima takes about the same time as flying coast to coast in your own country, and nobody thinks twice about a cross-country flight. Peru just feels farther because it's in another hemisphere. On the clock, it isn't.

And it doesn't cost a fortune

The other myth is that flights to South America are wildly expensive. They're not, especially if you live near a major airport. Cities like Miami, Atlanta, Los Angeles and Austin all have regular, competitively priced routes to Lima. If you time it reasonably, a round trip to Peru can run about the same as a round-trip domestic ticket in the States. So the barrier most people imagine, huge cost and huge time commitment, mostly isn't there. It's a normal flight, at a normal price.

Landing in a brand-new airport

Here's something even a lot of frequent travelers don't know yet: in June 2025, Lima opened a brand-new terminal at Jorge Chávez International Airport. It's three times the size of the old one, built as part of a roughly two-billion-dollar expansion, and it's now one of the largest and most modern terminals in South America, eventually handling up to forty million passengers a year.

It has around-the-clock restaurants and services, which matters a lot if you're catching a connecting flight onward to Cusco or into the jungle. The old Lima airport had a bit of a reputation for being cramped and chaotic. That's over. Now your arrival into Peru, and any connection from there, is smooth and comfortable.

You don't need two months. You need a long weekend.

So let go of the idea that seeing Peru is some massive, once-in-a-lifetime expedition you have to save up years for. You can fly down on a Thursday, spend a few days actually walking the land, seeing the towns, tasting the food, and be home by Monday if you want.

It's the same flight time as crossing your own country, at a comparable price, landing in a beautiful new airport. When you're ready to come see it for yourself, that first trip is a lot easier to make than you'd ever guess.
