Glossary

The words that show up in a purchase, in the language they are actually used. This is a phrase book, not a legal dictionary.

No term matches. Try a shorter word.

  • Abogado

    A lawyer. Yours should be independent of the seller and independent of me. I am not one.

  • Alcabala

    The property transfer tax, commonly described as 3% of the price above an exempt amount. The exemption changes and is often tied to the UIT. Confirm the current figure. The buyer usually pays it.

  • ANA

    Autoridad Nacional del Agua, the national water authority. Rural ditches and water rights often lead here. A hose in a field is not the same as a right.

  • Apostilla

    An apostille: the certificate that makes certain foreign public documents usable in Peru. One way a power of attorney signed abroad gets recognized. A consulate is the other common path. Your lawyer says which you need.

  • Arbitrios

    Municipal service charges, separate from property tax. Trash, parks, local security, and similar. They are billed locally and they can be unpaid by a seller.

  • Arras

    Earnest money under a promise to buy. What you lose if you walk away, and what happens if the title fails, has to be written. A transfer in a chat is not arras.

  • Autoconstrucción

    A building put up without a formal engineer’s project. Common, and a different seismic and wiring risk from engineered work. I can see the signs. An engineer judges them.

  • Autoavalúo

    The municipality’s valuation of a property, used in calculating predial. It is not the same number as the price you negotiated.

  • Bancarización

    The rule that payments above a threshold should go through the banking system. The threshold is set in UIT and changes. The bank trail is how you prove the price later.

  • Carné de extranjería

    The ID card for a foreigner with resident status. Buying a house does not issue one. Banks and notaries treat it differently from a tourist passport.

  • Certificado de gravámenes

    A SUNARP certificate of burdens on a property: mortgages, embargoes, and other claims. Read a fresh one close to signing.

  • Certificado literal

    A certified copy of the current registry entry: the description of the property, the registered owner, and the annotations on that file.

  • Cisterna

    A ground-level or buried water tank. Many houses store water because network pressure is not continuous.

  • COFOPRI

    The state body associated with formalizing informal property, especially urban. A path toward title, not a shortcut, and not a sure thing.

  • Comunidad campesina

    A rural peasant community that holds land collectively. An individual who offers you a piece may not have the right to sell it. Often the wrong purchase if you want a clean private title.

  • Comunidad nativa

    An indigenous community, often in the Amazon, holding land collectively. The same caution as comunidad campesina, with its own legal regime. Do not treat a handshake as a subdivision.

  • Conformidad de obra

    The municipality’s confirmation that finished construction matches the license. Missing paperwork means the building you see may not be the building the city recognizes.

  • Constancia de posesión

    A document saying someone possesses a property. Common in rural and jungle areas. It is not a registered title.

  • Copropietario

    A co-owner. All of them generally have to sign a sale unless one has real authority to sign for the rest.

  • Declaratoria de fábrica

    Registration of the building on the title. Without it, you may own registered land and an unregistered house.

  • Discoteca

    A nightclub. A classic reason a Saturday noon visit lies about noise.

  • El Niño

    A Pacific climate cycle that can bring severe flooding to Peru’s north coast. Not every year. A dry visit does not retire the risk.

  • Escritura pública

    The public deed signed before a notary. It is the formal sale document. It still has to be registered at SUNARP.

  • Garúa

    Lima’s gray coastal drizzle and overcast winter, roughly May through October. Little real rain, plenty of damp.

  • Gravamen

    A burden on the title: a mortgage, an embargo, or another registered claim. The certificado de gravámenes is the list.

  • Guardián

    A caretaker who watches a property. Oversight of that relationship is something I can do. It is not a property-management company.

  • Habilitación urbana

    The formal process of turning land into an urban subdivision with approved lots and services. A painted street is not always a habilitación.

  • Hipoteca

    A mortgage registered on the property. It should be cancelled or otherwise dealt with before you think you own clear title.

  • Huaico

    A debris flow: mud, rock, and water down a ravine, typical of Andean rainy seasons. A dry quebrada in July can be a huaico path in February.

  • Independización

    Splitting one registered property into separate titles. Until it is done, you may be buying a share of a larger file, not the lot you walked.

  • Licencia de construcción

    The municipal building permit. Building first and asking later is common and can freeze a later sale.

  • Maestro de obra

    The site foreman who runs day-to-day construction. Essential, and not a substitute for an engineer or architect on a real project.

  • Minuta

    The contract drafted, usually by a lawyer, for the notary to turn into the escritura pública. This is where names, price, and conditions should be exact.

  • Mototaxi

    A motorcycle taxi. In many towns it is the transport system and the sound of the street.

  • Notario

    A notary public. In Peru the notary formalizes the deed. The notary is not your advocate. Your abogado is.

  • Parámetros urbanísticos

    The municipal rules for a lot: land use, height, setbacks, and similar limits. They decide whether your sketch is legal.

  • Partida registral

    The registry file number of a property at SUNARP. It is how you find the record. It is not, by itself, proof that a particular person standing in the doorway may sell.

  • Plano

    A plan or map of the lot. The physical fences, the plano, and the registry description are three things. They should match.

  • Poder

    A power of attorney. For a remote purchase, limit it to one property and one transaction, with an end date. Consulate or apostille are the usual routes. I am not providing a form.

  • Posesión

    Possession: physical control of a property. It can exist with title, without title, or in conflict with the registered owner.

  • Predial

    Impuesto predial, the annual municipal property tax. Unpaid years belong in the negotiation, not in your first surprise bill.

  • Prescripción adquisitiva

    A legal path to title based on long, qualifying possession. It takes time and money and it can fail. Not a DIY project and not a selling point I will dress up.

  • Quebrada

    A ravine. Pretty when dry. The place huaicos use when it rains.

  • Rentista

    A residence category associated with qualifying pension or income. It is not granted by a property purchase. An immigration lawyer says whether you fit.

  • Se vende

    “For sale,” often painted on a wall with a phone number. A lead, not a contract.

  • Separación de patrimonios

    A marital regime in which property is held separately, unlike the default community regime. Which regime applies changes who must sign. The lawyer checks it.

  • Servidumbre

    An easement: a registered right to cross land or to run water across it. Access that is only a neighbor’s kindness is not this.

  • Sociedad conyugal

    The community-property side of a marriage, often discussed as sociedad de gananciales. Both spouses usually must sign to sell. A missing spouse is a broken sale.

  • Sol

    The Peruvian currency (PEN). Prices are agreed in soles or dollars. Write down which, and which exchange rate, if a conversion is involved. This site’s calculator does not convert.

  • Sucesión intestada

    An intestate succession: the owner died without a will the process is following, or the estate is being opened that way. All heirs have to be in it. One relative cannot sell the family’s property alone.

  • SUNARP

    The national public registry. Where title, mortgages, and many other rights are recorded. If it is not here, a later buyer may not have to care what a private paper says.

  • SUNAT

    The national tax authority. Not the land registry. Predial is municipal. Seller’s income tax and bancarización questions live nearer SUNAT. Do not mix the two offices up.

  • Tanque elevado

    An elevated tank, often on the roof, that gives a house pressure. Pair it in your mind with the cisterna.

  • Testimonio

    The notary’s official copy of the escritura. You will be asked for it when you move utilities or register. It is still not a substitute for the SUNARP entry.

  • Topógrafo

    A surveyor. The person you want when the fence, the plano, and the registry disagree.

  • UIT

    Unidad Impositiva Tributaria, a tax unit the state resets. Alcabala exemptions and bancarización thresholds are often expressed as a number of UIT. Look up the current UIT rather than trusting an old blog.

  • Usucapión

    Another way people refer to gaining ownership through long possession. In practice, read prescripción adquisitiva and talk to a lawyer. I am not offering a how-to.

  • Zona de frontera

    The border zone. Foreigners generally cannot own property within 50 km of Peru’s borders without special authorization. Measure the coordinates. Do not guess from a town name.

Home-country words that are not Peruvian

FBAR and FATCA are United States reporting regimes for foreign accounts. They are not Peruvian taxes. Thresholds change. A cross-border accountant is the person who says what you file. Rental income can be reportable at home as well as in Peru.
