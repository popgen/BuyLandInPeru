Why I do this

I bought land here the hard way, so you don't have to.

In 2018 I came to Peru planning to travel all of South America. I stayed four months, and spent one of them in a rented house in the central Peruvian Rainforest, waking up to parrots and a huge starfruit tree outside the window. I promised myself I'd come back and build a place in the jungle.

In 2021 I did. I bought a property about 3.5 km outside Lamas, at the end of a bumpy dirt road. The seller had to cut the borders with a machete just so I could see them. It was full of mangoes, bananas, zapote and caimito. It was also sold as having electricity, which turned out to be cables and meters from a government project that still wasn't finished years later.

That's the honest version of buying land here: it's beautiful, it's affordable, and it rewards people who check what actually works, speak the language, and take the time to know their neighbors.

How it works

You make every decision. I do the legwork on the ground.

Most of Peru's property market isn't online. Deals happen through conversations, WhatsApp messages and neighbors who know who's selling.

  1. Free first call

    We talk about what you want, where, and your budget. I tell you honestly what's realistic.

  2. I scout and film

    I (or my team) visit properties, walk the borders, and send you photos and video of what's really there.

  3. We check everything

    Title history in SUNARP, water and power that actually work, access in rainy season, and what the neighbors say.

  4. I negotiate

    In Spanish, face to face, at the local price rather than the foreigner price.

  5. You close safely

    Through a notary, with funds held by a bank trust or the notary, and the title registered in your name.

Your money

Your money never touches my hands

Being careful about buying land abroad is smart. Here's how every purchase is set up so you never have to take my word for it.

Held by a bank or notary

Your purchase money sits in a fideicomiso (a formal bank trust) or with the notary, and is released only when the agreed conditions are met. My fee is always separate.

Title in your name only

The property is registered with SUNARP, Peru's public registry, in your name. I'm never an owner, co-owner or title holder.

Everything in writing

A signed services agreement, a narrowly limited power of attorney if you buy remotely, and every document shared with you.

Getting here

Peru is closer than you think

You don't need two months off to come see land here. A nonstop flight to Lima takes about as long as flying across the United States, and from hubs like Miami, Atlanta, Los Angeles and Austin, a round trip often costs about the same as a domestic one.

Lima's new Jorge Chávez terminal opened in June 2025: three times bigger than the old one, with restaurants open around the clock. A long weekend is enough.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Can a foreigner really own property in Peru?

Yes. Peru's Constitution gives foreigners the same property rights as citizens, so you can own land, houses and apartments in your own name. The exception is within 50 km of an international border, which requires government approval. Outside those zones, which is most of the country, you own it outright.

How do I know this isn't a scam?

It's the right question. I never take your purchase money, every purchase goes through a licensed notary, and the title is registered in your name with SUNARP. I'm happy to video call, show you my own property, and connect you with past clients.

Do I have to travel to Peru to buy?

No, though I recommend visiting if you can. Remote purchases work through a limited power of attorney signed before a notary in your country, apostilled and translated, allowing your representative to sign only for the property and price you approve.

How much does the property itself cost?

Often far less than people expect. Small plots in edge-of-town developments can start in the low thousands of dollars, and rural acreage can be very affordable. Big cities like Lima cost much more. I'll give you honest numbers for what you have in mind.
