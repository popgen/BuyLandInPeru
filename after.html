After the purchase

The day after the deed is a different job.

If you live in another country, an empty house in Peru needs a person. I can oversee check-ins and help you understand the work. I do not run a property-management company, and I do not pretend a caretaker is a hotel.

Someone on the ground while you are not

A guardián watches a house: locks, a leak, a neighbor’s call, the plants, a broken window after a storm. Security is lights, a gate, and a person who notices, not a fantasy of a guard force. Bills still arrive. Predial and arbitrios do not pause because you are abroad. I can set a cadence of photos and storm check-ins. The fee for that oversight is quoted after we know the house and the distance. It is not a published package price.

Building and renovating

Work runs through a maestro de obra, and it should also run through an engineer or an architect when the job is more than a patch. The maestro is on site. The engineer is how you avoid guessing about a beam in an earthquake. Materials arrive when they arrive. A week of rain stops masonry. A price quoted in a hallway will move when the steel is purchased.

I can oversee in the sense of showing up, comparing the work with the plan, and telling you what I see. I am not the builder and I am not the engineer of record. Informal autoconstrucción is how a lot of Peru was built. It is also how cracks and wiring become your problem. If you are holding land to build later, budget the construction as its own project. I will not print a dollar-per-square-meter rate as fact.

Utilities in your name

Water, electricity, internet, and gas, where there is a gas network, need to move into the new owner’s name. Companies ask for the deed or a registration, an ID they accept, and sometimes the previous bills. A tourist passport can be a harder conversation than a carné. I will not promise a same-day transfer. Budget time, and keep the lights on with the seller’s account only under a written agreement so you are not depending on a stranger’s goodwill.

Renting it out

Short stays and long leases are different businesses, and municipal rules are local. Some buildings forbid short rentals. Some districts care. A platform listing is not permission. If you want income, your lawyer checks the rules where the property sits, and you may need a local person who actually manages guests or a tenant. I can help you see what that involves. I am not offering myself as a licensed rental administrator, and I am not promising occupancy.

Settling in

The useful first month is dull: a doctor, a dentist, the market you will actually use, a mechanic, and a Spanish class if you need one. I can point at the kind of place, and I will not publish a directory of names I have not asked to be listed.

Neighbors, fiestas, and noise are the culture, not an interruption of it. Show up as a neighbor who greets people, pays what is due, and does not treat the street as staff. The fastest way to be “the rich gringo” is to negotiate every small thing in public, skip the local custom, and talk about how cheap their country is. You can be foreign and still be decent. Language helps more than a higher offer.

Moving your life here

Shipping a household means a customs process, delays, and duties that can erase the romance. Many people replace furniture here. Pets and vehicles are often not worth importing: quarantine and paperwork for animals, and Peruvian rules plus the state of the road for a car that was cheap at home. Bring medicines you need with the documents a pharmacy or a doctor will accept, and then learn the local equivalent with a clinician. I am not a customs broker or a physician.

If plans change, selling is possible. It requires the same paper you were careful about on the way in, and it is not guaranteed. See selling later.
