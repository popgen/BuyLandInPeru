Immigration, at a high level

Buying a house does not let you stay.

People assume the deed is a visa. It is not. Residency is a separate process with its own rules, and those rules change. Nothing here is immigration advice or a path you can follow from a blog.

What a purchase does not do

A registered property does not give you residency, a work permit, or citizenship. I became a Peruvian citizen later, after a life here. That was an immigration process. It was not a prize for having bought land. I will not publish a do-it-yourself citizenship guide, because I am not qualified to maintain one and because my file is not your file.

Categories people ask about

These are names, not instructions. An immigration lawyer is the person who says whether you fit.

  • Rentista. A residence path associated with a steady pension or other qualifying income. It is a common question for retirees. The income test and the paperwork are theirs to confirm.
  • Investor. A category tied to an investment that meets a threshold and a set of conditions. Buying an ordinary house is not automatically that category.
  • Work. Based on a job and an employer’s process, not on a property.
  • Family. Based on a qualifying relationship with a Peruvian or a resident.

A carné de extranjería is the foreigner ID card that comes with a resident status. It is not something the notary prints because you bought an apartment.

Overstaying

A tourist stay has a limit. Overstaying can mean fines and trouble the next time you enter or the next time you apply for something real. I am not describing how to manage an overstay. Leave on time or change status properly. If you are unsure of your date, ask an immigration lawyer or the authority, not a property guide.

Tourist stamp, carné, and citizen

I bought property when I was still a foreigner coming and going, and I bought again after I naturalized. The purchase did not change my immigration status in either case. What felt different, in ordinary life, was administration. Banks, longer stays, and signing as a citizen were not the same experience as arranging documents as a visitor. A person with a carné de extranjería sits between those two: resident ID, not citizenship.

I am not going to invent the procedural steps of my own file as if they were current instructions. If you are on a tourist stamp, ask your property lawyer what identification the notary will accept for the escritura, and ask an immigration lawyer about how long you can stay. Those are different appointments.

The border-zone limit on foreign ownership is one place citizenship mattered in substance. It is described on the legal page. It is still not a reason to buy something in the hope of becoming Peruvian.
